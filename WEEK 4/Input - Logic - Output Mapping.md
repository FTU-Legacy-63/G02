## 3. Input - Logic - Output Mapping

### 3.1 Main Flow

| Phase | Player Input | Logic / Rule | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 - Examination & Vitals | Player hears David's opening framing at Màn 1 (Phòng khám): sales decline, Amazon/Walmart/online pressure, cost cuts, digital investment — plus a hint of family conflict (Michael's lawyer, Richard quietly shopping international assets). | Present financial symptoms and family-conflict hints without giving the root cause. A hidden governance subplot (Michael's conditional Houndstooth Capital agreement) exists but stays unconfirmed until HS-G is opened. | Player may suspect competitive/operational pressure, capital structure, or family governance, but evidence is incomplete until dossiers are opened at Màn 2. |
| Phase 2 - Deep Diagnostics | Player picks 3 of 7 dossiers (HS-A…HS-G) at Màn 2, 2 of 7 follow-up questions (CH1-A…CH1-G) at Màn 3, reviews the full financial statements at Màn 4, then 1 of 7 final questions (CH2-A…CH2-G) at Màn 5. | Each dossier/question has materiality. The 4 dossiers not chosen at Màn 2 close permanently — they cannot be reopened at Màn 3, 4, 5, or during re-examination at Màn 11. Opening HS-G is required to unlock the Houndstooth/family-conflict evidence. | Sales & customer data, stores/CapEx backlog, liquidity position, capital structure & leverage history, product/category trends, management track record, and/or governance & family evidence, depending on choices made. |
| Diagnosis | Player selects 1 of 6 diagnoses (CD-A…CD-F) at Màn 6, each presented with its strongest supporting evidence. | Diagnoses are tied to specific dossiers/questions (e.g., CD-D needs HS-D). CD-F (Mixed Disease) requires HS-D plus at least one other related dossier; picking it without that coverage triggers a Uy tín (Credibility) −1 penalty. | Diagnosis result; unlocked/narrowed treatment protocols at Màn 7; possible Credibility adjustment. |
| Phase 3 - Treatment | Player selects 1 of 6 treatment protocols (PD-A…PD-F) at Màn 7, 1 of 4 timings (TG-A…TG-D) at Màn 8, and 3 of 7 priority groups at Màn 9. | PD-A requires at least medium Credibility (else auto-downgrades to PD-B); PD-F requires high Credibility; PD-C's success depends heavily on prioritizing Creditors. No timing or priority choice is risk-free. | A combined protocol + timing + preparation package that feeds the Màn 10 event resolver. |
| Follow-up / Consequence | Event resolver at Màn 10 applies 7 update tabs (Sales, Vendor, Liquidity, Store, Digital, Debt, Family/Board) based on the Màn 7–9 choices; player may revisit diagnosis/treatment at Màn 11 (Tái khám). | Missing supplier/creditor preparation, poor timing (e.g., TG-D), or ignoring Governance & Family can trigger vendor cash-terms demands, inventory shortfalls, or a family vote against the player. Changing diagnosis or treatment at Màn 11 without genuinely new evidence costs Credibility. | Updated Sales/Vendor/Liquidity/Store/Digital/Debt/Family status; possible Credibility penalty; confirmed or revised diagnosis and treatment. |
| Final Screen | — (Màn 12 resolves automatically from all prior choices). | Financial Resilience is scored via a point formula (protocol, timing, and preparation events); Governance Stability is scored via a separate point formula (HS-G, priority choices, Credibility losses); both are converted into tiers and placed on a 3×3 outcome matrix. Credibility (Low/Medium/High) colors the narrative tone rather than changing the matrix cell. | Player sees the outcome matrix cell (Financial Resilience tier × Governance Stability tier), the matching ending narrative, and a case summary (dossiers opened, questions asked, diagnosis/treatment before and after re-exam, timing, priorities, final Credibility, both score tiers, final outcome). |

### 3.2 Evidence-To-Logic Mapping

| Evidence / Input | Logic It Supports | Expected Output |
|---|---|---|
| FY2026 Operating Earnings = $460m, up from $378m in FY2025 (income statement, Màn 4). | Operating performance improved despite the net loss. | Supports Capital Structure / Financing diagnosis (CD-D); contradicts a diagnosis of pure competitive/operational collapse (CD-A / CD-B). |
| Interest Expense ~$429–457m across FY2024–FY2026 (HS-D / income statement, Màn 4). | Interest largely absorbs the operating gain, keeping Earnings Before Tax near breakeven ($5m in FY2026). | Explains why Net Loss attributable to the company (−$36m FY2026) persists despite Operating Earnings improving. |
| Consolidated Same-Store Sales = −1.4% in FY2026 (HS-A). | Sales pressure exists but is not catastrophic on its own. | Treats competitive/e-commerce pressure (CD-A) as a comorbidity rather than the sole cause once capital-structure evidence (HS-D) is also on the table. |
| Adjusted EBITDA = $792m in FY2026 (HS-D). | The business still generates a solid operating cash-flow proxy before the debt burden. | Supports a "not operationally dead" interpretation; keeps Liquidity Disease (CD-C) secondary rather than primary. |
| 2015 leveraged buyout of $6.6bn → current Total Indebtedness ~$4.8bn / Secured Indebtedness ~$3.4bn (HS-D). | Current leverage traces to a single financing event, not gradual market erosion. | Supports Capital Structure / Financing Disease (CD-D) as the root cause rather than CD-A (Competitive) or CD-E (Product & Category). |
| Pre-LBO Operating Cash Flow: $575m (2012) / $801m (2013) / $746m (2014) (HS-D). | The company generated healthy operating cash flow before the 2015 LBO. | Supports a "healthy before the debt event" narrative, with the caveat that pre-2015 figures predate current competitive conditions. |
| Executing a protocol before the holiday season with no Suppliers/Holiday Inventory priority (TG-A + Màn 9 choices). | Even the correct treatment protocol can fail if timing and preparation are wrong. | Applies a negative Financial Resilience / Vendor Update modifier to protocols such as PD-C (Debt Restructuring) or PD-E (Bankrupt with Reorganization). |
| Supplier/vendor terms and the Suppliers/Creditors priority choices (HS-C; Màn 9–10). | Retail survival depends on inventory availability and vendor trust. | Skipping Suppliers/Creditors priority can trigger vendor cash-in-advance demands and holiday inventory shortfalls in the Vendor Update tab. |
| Michael's conditional Houndstooth Capital agreement and Richard's asset-sale intentions (HS-G; CH1-G / CH2-G). | Family stakeholders' incentives may be conflicted and can be used against the player. | Feeds the Governance Stability axis and CD-F eligibility; not used for direct Financial Resilience scoring. |

### 3.3 Sample Input - Logic - Output

| Scenario | Input | Logic | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premature diagnosis | Player selects CD-A (Competitive / E-commerce Disease) at Màn 6 after opening none of the 7 dossiers at Màn 2. | Evidence coverage is zero; player accepts David's opening framing at face value. | Credibility penalty and feedback that a counselor should not diagnose from client testimony alone; treatment options at Màn 7 skew toward the protocol matching the unverified diagnosis. |
| Correct diagnosis | Player opens HS-A and HS-D at Màn 2, asks CH1-D at Màn 3, reviews the full financials at Màn 4, and selects CD-D (Capital Structure / Financing Disease) at Màn 6. | Evidence shows Operating Earnings improved ($378m → $460m), leverage traces to the 2015 LBO, and SSS decline is mild (−1.4%). | Strong, evidence-backed diagnosis with no Credibility penalty; all six treatment protocols (PD-A…PD-F) remain available, subject to the Credibility gates on PD-A and PD-F. |
| Bankruptcy/reorganization with bad timing | Player selects PD-E (Bankrupt with Reorganization) at Màn 7, TG-A (immediate execution) at Màn 8, and does not prioritize Suppliers or Holiday Inventory at Màn 9. | PD-E without Suppliers priority applies a −2 Financial Resilience penalty; TG-A is based on incomplete holiday data and gives Michael grounds to challenge the plan. | Financial Resilience score drops sharply, risking the "Sụp đổ tài chính" (financial collapse) tier; the Vendor Update tab shows suppliers demanding cash-in-advance and holiday inventory shortages. |
| Bankruptcy/reorganization with preparation | Player selects PD-E at Màn 7, prioritizes Suppliers (and ideally Holiday Inventory) at Màn 9, and avoids TG-D (the 6-month wait). | PD-E with Suppliers priority earns +1 Financial Resilience instead of −2, and avoiding TG-D avoids its −1 penalty. | Financial Resilience improves meaningfully, giving restructuring a credible path to the "Sống sót mong manh" or "Sống sót vững" tier. |
| Operational cuts only | Player selects PD-B (Deep Cost Cutting) alone. | PD-B lifts short-term cash (+3 Financial Resilience) but does not touch the leverage/interest burden and shrinks the long-term sales base. | Short-term improvement is a false positive; if the root cause was really capital structure (CD-D), long-term outcome quality still suffers because the underlying disease is untreated. |

### 3.4 Expected Path A - Good Counselor

| Step | Player Input | Expected Result |
|---|---|---|
| Investigation | Opens HS-A, HS-D (and ideally HS-C) at Màn 2, asks CH1-D at Màn 3, reviews the full financial statements at Màn 4. | High evidence coverage. |
| Diagnosis | Selects CD-D (Capital Structure / Financing Disease) at Màn 6. | Strong diagnosis reasoning; no Credibility penalty. |
| Evidence cited | Operating Earnings improved to $460m, interest expense (~$450–457m) absorbs most of the gain, leverage traces to the 2015 LBO (~$4.8bn total debt), SSS decline is mild (−1.4%). | Evidence-backed diagnosis confirmed. |
| Treatment | Selects PD-C (Debt Restructuring) or PD-E (Bankrupt with Reorganization), avoids TG-A and TG-D, and prioritizes Creditors and Suppliers at Màn 9. | High suitability, because both root cause and execution risk are addressed. |
| Follow-up | Updates the plan at Màn 11 (Tái khám) only when Màn 10 reveals genuinely new evidence. | No unnecessary Credibility loss. |
| Final | High Credibility; strong Financial Resilience tier; Governance Stability holds if Governance & Family was also prioritized — landing near "Người kế nghiệp" or "Cầm cự cùng nhau" on the 3×3 matrix. |

### 3.5 Expected Path B - Poor Information / Wrong Diagnosis

| Step | Player Input | Expected Result |
|---|---|---|
| Investigation | Opens only HS-A (Sales & Customer) at Màn 2, skipping capital-structure evidence. | Low evidence coverage. |
| Diagnosis | Selects CD-A (Competitive / E-commerce Disease) at Màn 6. | Weak diagnosis reasoning due to missing HS-D (Capital Structure & Leverage History). |
| Treatment | Selects PD-B (Deep Cost Cutting) alone. | Short-term cash improves, but the leverage/interest burden remains untouched. |
| Follow-up | Ignores the continuing interest burden and does not revisit the diagnosis at Màn 11. | No correction made; low adaptation. |
| Final | Feedback explains the counselor "treated symptoms, not the disease"; Financial Resilience likely lands in the "Sống sót mong manh" tier or worse. |

### 3.6 Expected Path C - Correct Tool, Bad Execution

| Step | Player Input | Expected Result |
|---|---|---|
| Investigation | Opens HS-D and discovers the capital-structure problem. | Good investigation and diagnosis (CD-D). |
| Treatment | Selects PD-E (Bankrupt with Reorganization) or PD-C (Debt Restructuring) at Màn 7. | Potentially suitable protocol. |
| Execution | Chooses TG-A (immediate execution) at Màn 8 and skips the Suppliers priority at Màn 9. | Timing/preparation modifiers turn negative (e.g., PD-E without Suppliers priority = −2 Financial Resilience). |
| Consequence | Vendor Update shows suppliers demanding cash terms; Sales/Store Updates show holiday inventory problems. | Financial Resilience falls despite the correct diagnosis. |
| Final | Game teaches "right diagnosis, wrong timing or preparation can still fail." |
