## 1. Role in the Project

In this project, I served as the **UI/UX Designer and Dialogue Writer**, responsible for user interface design and visual experience representation for Medifin - a financial consulting simulation game. My role focused on layout organization, information hierarchy, and building a cohesive visual style across the interface.

Specifically, I used the game's user flow as a baseline to design screens for each game stage, built interactive prototypes, structured information presentation, and curated choices as well as character content on every screen.

## 2. Personal Mark on the Product

* **Visual Direction & Brand Identity:** Developed a comprehensive conceptual proposal on Canva that served as the foundational style tile for the entire project. This deck established the aesthetic framework - including psychological color mapping (dark blue for financial trust and focus), structured grid layouts, scalable character archetypes, and narrative pacing - enabling the cross-functional team to achieve total design alignment before touching high-fidelity wireframes.

* **Cohesive Game UI & Design System:** Architected a modular, scalable UI component library applied consistently across all game levels. Key features included an immersive dark-blue atmospheric background contrasted with high-legibility, light-toned content containers, ergonomic rounded card geometry, and a rigid typographic hierarchy that optimized cognitive load during complex financial reading tasks.

* **Interactive Prototyping & Flow Architecture:** Engineered a high-fidelity, clickable prototype in Figma that mapped out multi-branched user journeys. By establishing complex component states, Smart Animate transitions, and trigger-based navigation, the prototype successfully simulated end-to-end player progression, decision branching points, and feedback loops across every stage of the simulation.

* **Narrative Integration & Contextual UI:** Seamlessly wove storytelling elements into functional UI components by embedding contextual environmental backgrounds and dynamic character dialogue boxes directly into the gameplay loop. This bridged the gap between dry financial data and emotional engagement, transforming raw economic metrics into a compelling narrative experience where players felt the real-world weight of their advisory decisions.

## 8. Challenges Faced & Solutions

* **Challenge 1: Unfamiliarity with Figma Tools**
  * **Issue:** Having limited prior experience with Figma, I initially struggled with UI operations and prototyping tools.
  * **Solution:** I proactively learned through online tutorials - ranging from layout techniques and frame alignment to button interactions and screen linking. Through practice, I became proficient and efficient with the software.

* **Challenge 2: Repetitive Interactive Patterns Across Levels**
  * **Issue:** Many game levels originally had similar structures centered on reading profiles, evaluating options, and making choices. Keeping interactions identical while only changing content risked making the experience feel monotonous.
  * **Solution:** I added character dialogue at appropriate stages, fostering human connection and emotional engagement between characters and players rather than presenting a static screen.

* **Challenge 3: Presenting Dense Financial Data Without Overwhelming Players**
  * **Issue:** Many screens had to show financial statements, ratios, and company metrics at the same time. Placing everything in one view made the screens crowded and hard to scan, especially for players with limited finance background.
  * **Solution:** I applied progressive disclosure and a strict information hierarchy. Key figures were grouped into structured data cards, secondary details were placed in separate info panels, and consistent typography and spacing guided the eye. I also worked with the Financial Content Lead to decide which metrics were essential on each screen.

* **Challenge 4: Balancing Text-Heavy Content with Readability**
  * **Issue:** Scenario descriptions, profiles, and character dialogue contained a lot of text, which could tire players or cause them to skip important clues.
  * **Solution:** I used readable font sizes, clear contrast between dark backgrounds and light content containers, short dialogue lines, and clearly separated sections. Important choices and clues were visually emphasized so players could find them quickly.

## 9. Advice for Future Students

For teams continuing the development of this project, UI and UX should be treated as core elements of the product. UI ensures that information, characters, and choices are clearly presented, while UX guides how players process information, make decisions, and observe outcomes. Designing these two aspects in sync allows players to focus on game content and understand the significance of each step.

Start addressing UI/UX early alongside content creation and rule design. Consider what players need to see on each screen, what the next action is, and whether the interface clearly communicates those steps. A well-structured experience makes the product accessible, coherent, and far more effective at delivering the game's objectives.
