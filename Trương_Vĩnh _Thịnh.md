# 1. Introduction & Individual Role

**MEDIFIN – Financial Clinic** is an interactive, web-based financial simulation developed by Group 02. Through the case of *Toy Kingdom Inc.*, players take the role of financial consultants, investigate corporate financial difficulties, propose strategic solutions, and observe how their decisions influence the company's future.

The project combines financial analysis, interactive storytelling, and decision-based gameplay to create a practical learning experience.

My assigned role was **Frontend & Integration Developer**. However, my involvement extended beyond frontend programming to include initial game ideation, gameplay structure, technical refinement, and final project integration.

### Key Responsibilities

- **Independent Development:** Built MEDIFIN Versions 1 and 3.
- **Game Ideation:** Contributed initial ideas for the round structure, player journey, and decision-making experience.
- **Collaborative Development:** Worked with Lê Bảo Ngọc to refine Version 4 into the Final Upgrade.
- **Final Integration:** Reviewed, corrected, consolidated, and synchronized the team's components into the final application.
- **GitHub Management:** Organized and published the integrated project and its supporting materials.

Although my work involved financial and gameplay-related discussions, my primary responsibility remained **technical development and integration**.

# 3. Game Ideation & Interdisciplinary Collaboration

## 3.1. Initial Game Concept & User Flow

In addition to programming, I contributed ideas during the early conceptual development of MEDIFIN.

I proposed how the financial consulting simulation could be structured, including the number and sequence of rounds, the progression of the player's experience, and the relationship between financial investigation and strategic decision-making.

My suggestions focused on:

- **Round Structure:** Dividing the consulting process into interactive stages.
- **Player Journey:** Guiding players from identifying financial problems to proposing solutions and evaluating consequences.
- **Decision-Based Learning:** Encouraging players to apply financial knowledge through choices rather than passive reading.

These were conceptual contributions that informed the team's discussions. The detailed user flow, scenario development, and game logic were subsequently developed and refined collaboratively.

## 4.2. Challenges Encountered & Solutions

### Challenge 1: Translating UI/UX Designs into Functional Gameplay

**Challenge:** The visual designs needed to be converted into interactive interfaces while maintaining consistency across different rounds.

**Solution:** I implemented the designs using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, refining layouts and interactions to balance visual presentation with functionality.

### Challenge 2: Maintaining Consistency Across Multiple Versions

**Challenge:** As Versions 1–4 were developed by different contributors at different stages, differences in file structure, interface behavior, and gameplay implementation created integration challenges.

**Solution:** I reviewed the updated components, identified inconsistencies, and synchronized the necessary files while preserving the original contributions of team members.

### Challenge 3: Connecting Financial Decisions with Technical Logic

**Challenge:** The game required player decisions, scoring rules, and final outcomes to remain logically consistent with the financial Decision Set.

**Solution:** I contributed to discussions about the decision-making framework and participated in reviewing its technical implementation, particularly during the Final Upgrade stage.

### Challenge 4: Managing and Publishing Working Versions

**Challenge:** Different development versions needed to remain accessible for testing, collaboration, and documentation.

**Solution:** I maintained separate working versions, organized the source files, and published the relevant builds through GitHub and GitHub Pages.

# 5. Personal Reflection & Learning Outcomes

## 5.1. What I Learned

Developing MEDIFIN provided an opportunity to apply programming skills to a project that combined financial analysis, interactive storytelling, and educational technology.

The experience helped me develop several important competencies:

- **Technical Skills:** Strengthened my practical knowledge of HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and interactive web development.
- **Problem-Solving:** Improved my ability to identify errors, troubleshoot inconsistencies, and refine interconnected components.
- **System Integration:** Developed a better understanding of how independently created modules can be combined into a functional application.
- **Collaboration:** Learned to coordinate with teammates working on financial content, UI/UX design, storytelling, and game logic.
- **Project Management:** Gained practical experience in organizing development versions, maintaining source code, and publishing applications through GitHub.

Most importantly, I learned that successful technical development involves more than writing functional code. It also requires understanding the broader project objectives and ensuring that different components work together effectively.

## 5.2. Personal Reflection

Looking back, my most meaningful contribution was my continuous involvement across several development stages.

I independently developed Versions 1 and 3, contributed ideas to the initial gameplay concept, and later collaborated on reviewing and refining Version 4.

The final integration stage was particularly valuable because it required me to consider the application as a complete system rather than a collection of separate features.

This experience strengthened my confidence in both independent technical development and collaborative problem-solving.

It also demonstrated how financial knowledge can be combined with technology to create more engaging and practical learning experiences.

## 5.3. Message to Future Students

For future students working on similar interdisciplinary projects, I would offer three suggestions.

**First, build a functional prototype early.** A working prototype helps the team evaluate ideas, identify technical limitations, and make improvements before the project becomes too complex.

**Second, maintain clear communication between different roles.** Financial content, UI/UX design, storytelling, and programming should be developed collaboratively rather than treated as completely separate tasks.

**Finally, prioritize integration and testing.** A project is not complete simply because its individual components are finished. The final product must be consistent, functional, and accessible to its intended users.

MEDIFIN taught me that the value of a group project lies not only in individual contributions but also in how effectively those contributions are brought together.
