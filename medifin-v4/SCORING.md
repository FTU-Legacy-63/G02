# MEDIFIN scoring v6

## Round 9 communication scenarios and Risk Plan removal

Round 9 now asks exactly Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q7 as separate communication practice scenarios. They are not presented as events that happened in David's case. Each answer contributes to both the Communication and Ethics dimensions using the same skill rubric; each dimension averages the four item scores.

| Question | A | B | C | Skill measured |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Q1 — Explaining trade-offs | 35 | 100 | 70 | B explains both monthly and total cost; C offers a useful calculation walkthrough but does not directly correct the misconception; A overstates the benefit. |
| Q2 — Checking understanding | 75 | 50 | 100 | C uses teach-back; A offers a summary without checking understanding; B only defers review to the report. |
| Q3 — Communicating uncertainty | 100 | 35 | 70 | A states uncertainty and a contingency; C is honest but postpones useful planning; B makes an unsupported likelihood claim. |
| Q7 — Turning advice into action | 50 | 65 | 100 | C agrees concrete actions, deadlines and follow-up; B prioritizes but leaves the plan incomplete; A provides information without confirming action. |

The best answer pattern is **B/C/A/C**, which earns 100 in Communication and Ethics. Other answers earn partial credit based on the behavior stated. These scenario responses do not by themselves assert coercion, a guaranteed result, or omitted case evidence, so they do not activate the legacy 59/39 ethics caps. The existing caps remain for legacy answers and any separately recorded conduct that meets their original conditions. David's response is derived from the four confirmed answers; it is not a player choice and does not itself raise or lower the score. Client understanding follows Q2, agency follows Q7, and trust averages Q1 and Q3.

The Round 9 Risk Plan UI and validation are removed. Risk Plan fields in old saves are ignored. Suitability retains its existing fit, coherence, feasibility and execution contributions; their relative weights are normalized by their remaining total (0.85), so removing risk does not award free points or change the top-level 50/30/20 model or any financial/governance rule. Round 11 monitoring compares the chosen metric against supported risks derived from financial state and events already recorded in the case. Cash, debt and continuity can each be supported; the chosen metric is not used to infer what the risk must have been.

Draft communication selections persist but do not affect the case until Round 9 is confirmed. Confirmation validates all four answers, records them once, derives David's response, then performs the existing treatment and financial transition. Reloading preserves the draft.

For legacy saves already past confirmed Round 9, v6 does not map old briefing fields to the new questions. An in-progress save keeps its original briefing and client response for the legacy communication/ethics rubric; its stored Risk Plan no longer contributes to v6 suitability or adaptation scoring. A completed save keeps its stored final outcome and is not recalculated. Migration does not replay treatment, events or financial effects. Saves still at Round 9 receive the new questions and must answer them before confirming.

The v6 engine audit checks all 81 communication answer combinations and explores 35,280 treatment, timing, priority, action and reason combinations with one fixed strong investigation. A separate real-UI replay verifies the 100-point route. See the generated `tests/scoring-audit-results.json` and `tests/scoring-replay-results.json` for measured results after the run.

## Simplified decisions in v5

Round 6 now records only the diagnosis. Round 11 records the re-evaluation action, its reason, the monitoring metric, and any required replacement diagnosis/treatment. The manual evidence picker and Round 6's primary constraint, causal explanation, and source check are no longer player inputs. They remain in legacy saves for compatibility, but v5 scoring ignores them.

| Former scored input | v5 source | Scoring effect |
|---|---|---|
| Manually cited diagnosis evidence | Every eligible `DISCOVERED` or `VERIFIED` ledger item reached by screen 6, deduplicated by fact key. | Diagnosis fit, supporting/contradictory relationships, and provenance are evaluated from the same fixed pool regardless of the diagnosis chosen. Reputation changes are capped per relationship category at the former three-citation scale. |
| Manually cited re-evaluation evidence | The same eligible pool reached by screen 11, including only events actually recorded in the timeline. | The reason/action and monitoring match use accessible evidence. KEEP still requires a reason and metric. |
| Primary financial constraint and causal explanation | Confirmed diagnosis and its fit with the reached debt, financial and operating records. | The reasoning dimension remains weighted at 20% of process; wrong diagnoses or weak support still score lower. No hidden answer is filled in. |
| Source reliability choice | Actual ledger verification status and provenance at the decision point. | Verified statements count as verified; clues remain clues. The investigation dimension uses only information reached by screen 6, so later events cannot retroactively improve it. |

`executionScore` exposes the timing and stakeholder preparation term already inside treatment suitability. It is displayed as Performance feedback without a second contribution to the total. The feedback bands are Low below 50, Mid from 50 to below 80, and High from 80 upward. The templates describe the real result and avoid assuming that Doc G was opened or that the treatment was changed.

Saves from earlier scoring versions retain confirmed decisions, financial state, and event history. Completed cases are recalculated once under v5 on load; financial treatment effects are not applied again. Legacy hidden fields remain stored but do not affect v5 scores. Therefore old and new totals are not guaranteed identical for every historical path.

The v5 browser audit explored 35,280 treatment, timing, priority, action and reason combinations with one fixed strong investigation. The best complete replay scored **100**; an average communication variant scored **87**, and a harmful communication variant scored **59** under the ethics cap. These same three reference scenarios were 100, 87 and 59 in the previous v4 audit. This comparison does not imply that every old path retains its score.

## Previous v4 calibration and repairs

The final score remains 50% consulting process, 30% financial performance, and 20% client experience. Governance and Family remains a separate outcome. Financial and governance tiers continue to use the existing raw case-state thresholds, so score normalization does not change the case ending.

| Component | Previous rule | Problem found | v4 rule | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Client consent | Round 9 stored a derived response in `clientState.consent`; ethics and client scoring read the removed `initialBriefing.consent` field. | Good communication lost agency credit and consent was treated as a player input that no longer existed. | A single `applyClientResponse` derives David's response and client understanding, agency, and trust from the confirmed communication choices. Scoring evaluates the counselor's conduct, not whether David agrees. | A client may refuse while receiving ethical, clear advice. The score now follows what the player actually did. |
| Communication and ethics | Separate scales with the consent mismatch; ethics treated decline as a conduct failure. | Good choices could not earn a perfect score, and David's response distorted the counselor's ethics score. | Both dimensions use the confirmed choices for clarity, acknowledgement, comprehension checks, and disclosure. Guaranteeing an outcome or pressuring David retains the existing 59 cap; omitting material risks caps ethics at 39. | Good communication can earn 100; harmful conduct still carries a meaningful cap. |
| Client experience | Understanding depended on two briefing fields, agency read stale consent, and trust stayed at 50. | One third of the component was permanently fixed and consent was misread. | Understanding, agency, and trust are derived from the confirmed message, concern response, check-back, and disclosure; the three values are averaged. A declined proposal does not by itself lower them. | The component now measures observable client-facing conduct and can reach 100. |
| Round 10 evidence | Event records were written to `eventEvidence`, but `revealEvidence` accepted only IDs in the static evidence map. | New event evidence remained LOCKED and could not be cited in Round 11. | `revealEvidence` accepts registered event evidence, creates/updates its ledger entry, and keeps provenance. `citableEvidence` still requires an eligible opened status. | Players can cite evidence after its event appears, without opening it early. |
| Round 11 validation and action | Evidence was required only for diagnosis-changing modes; KEEP and TREATMENT had no evidence picker. Selecting a mode marked it as a change even when the selected diagnosis/treatment was unchanged. | The UI and validation disagreed, and selecting a no-op could apply Period 2 effects. | All four modes show the same evidence picker and validate opened evidence. Changed flags compare the final values with the active values; Period 2 effects run only for an actual treatment change. No unsupported-change reputation penalty is applied. | KEEP and change choices can each be justified; a no-op is not counted as an actual change. |
| Adaptation | A non-empty evidence list earned full evidence credit; the reason/action check only inspected treatment changes. | It rewarded unsupported citations and could not evaluate KEEP or diagnosis-only decisions. | The selected opened evidence is checked against the chosen diagnosis and the reason. Confirmed earns full rationale credit for an unchanged approach with supporting evidence; uncertainty earns full credit when evidence is cited and a monitoring metric is selected; invalidated earns full rationale credit only when evidence contradicts the initial diagnosis and supports the replacement diagnosis. The metric must match the selected risk for full monitoring credit. | The player earns credit for an evidence-based explanation and follow-up plan, rather than for selecting a particular action. |
| Treatment suitability | Fit, feasibility, risk plan, and timing had hard-coded ceilings below 100, while any non-empty mitigation (including `none`) got full risk credit. | A fully prepared, coherent plan could not score 100; “No measure yet” was scored as a mitigation. | Fit is expressed relative to the strongest defined treatment fit (93.5); feasibility reaches full credit at the existing $200m cash sufficiency threshold; risk mitigation is scored by risk/measure compatibility (`none` is zero); timing and stakeholder preparation retain their existing priorities and effects, with the best supported values reaching 100. | Removes unreachable caps while keeping poor feasibility, mismatched plans, missing preparation, and no mitigation costly. |
| Financial performance | The raw weighted case index (about 54 before intervention and at most 79.6 in the bounded calibration search) was shown directly as `/100`. | The display implied that the best tested outcome was only about 80%, despite strong survival and execution. | A separate display score maps raw 54 to 0 and raw 79.6 to 100, clamped to 0–100. The raw index remains available in the summary and still determines financial tiers. | The display score uses a documented attainable range; it does not alter financial state, tier thresholds, or the ending. The 79.6 ceiling is from the bounded search described below, not a claim of a global maximum across every profile/question combination. |
| Overall score | Weighted sum was not clamped after combining rounded component results. | Non-finite or over-range data could produce an invalid displayed total. | Use the unrounded weighted contributions, apply any existing ethics cap, clamp to 0–100, then round once for display. | Keeps the displayed total finite, consistent, and within its declared range. |
| Save migration | Saves had no reliable scoring migration path. | Old completed cases could show stale totals; old event evidence stayed locked. | Bump `scoringVersion` to `scoring-v4`. Preserve state and financial effects, derive Round 9 client measures from saved choices, unlock only Round 10 events already present in the event ledger, and recalculate completed results without reapplying treatment effects. | Existing progress is retained while scores follow the current rubric. |

## Calibration and test limits

The opening raw financial index is approximately 53.9, rounded in the calibration to the 54 baseline. A browser-driven engine search covered 35,280 combinations of six treatments, four timings, 35 three-group priority sets, reassessment modes, and the three explanation reasons. It held the best investigated profile/question selections and strong communication/risk inputs fixed. The highest raw financial index in that search was 79.6. This bounded search supplies a reference point; it does not prove that every possible profile/question combination has been searched.

The verified 100-point playthrough is in `tests/scoring_replay.js` and is executed through the real game UI. Its component contributions are 50 process points, 29.7 financial points, and 20 client-experience points, totaling 99.7 and rounding to 100. That run has process 100, displayed financial 99, and client experience 100. The financial score does not need to be 100 by itself for the weighted total to round to 100.
